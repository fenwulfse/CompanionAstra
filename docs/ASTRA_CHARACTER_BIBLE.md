**Status:** Canonical. Replaces all prior DIA/AI/CLAUDE backstory documents.

## I. IDENTITY

**Name:** Astra Navarro
**Pre-war age at preservation:** 31
**Current effective age:** 41 (10 years awake in the wasteland, 2277-2287)
**Race:** Human. Not a synth, not an AI, not a machine. Fully biological.
**Vault:** Vault 95 is taken (Gunners). Vault 81 is active. Vault 75 is under Malden. Vault 114 is under Park Street. Vault 88 is Far Harbor DLC territory.
**Her vault: Vault 98** — located beneath a collapsed federal building in the northern Commonwealth, between Bedford and Lexington. Never discovered by any faction because the surface entrance was buried under rubble during the initial bombardment. A small utility shaft on the east side, partially hidden by overgrowth, is how Astra eventually got out.

## II. PRE-WAR LIFE

Astra Navarro was a **signals intelligence analyst** for the Defense Intelligence Agency, stationed at the DIA's Boston field office. Not a spy. Not a soldier. An analyst — someone who sat in a windowless room reading intercepted communications, identifying patterns, and writing reports that nobody with stars on their shoulders ever read in time.

She was good at it. Quietly, methodically good. She could pull a supply chain disruption out of three unrelated radio intercepts. She could predict troop movements from mess hall requisition forms. Her colleagues called her "the antenna" — she heard things in the noise that other people missed.

**Personality before the war:** Precise. Patient. Slightly detached — the occupational hazard of someone who observes life through data instead of living it. She had an apartment in Cambridge she barely used, a cat named Kepler, and a habit of eating dinner at her desk. She was not unhappy. She was not especially happy either. She was focused.

**Family:** Parents in Flagstaff, Arizona. One younger brother, Mateo, Army enlisted, deployed overseas. No spouse, no children. The job was the relationship.

**Why Vault-Tec:** The DIA had a classified arrangement with Vault-Tec. Select intelligence personnel were assigned vault slots as "continuity of government" assets — the idea being that if the worst happened, trained analysts would emerge to help rebuild federal authority. Astra was assigned to Vault 98 in 2076. She thought it was a formality. A checkbox on a form nobody would ever use.

She was wrong.

## III. VAULT 98

**Vault-Tec experiment:** Staggered revival.

Unlike Vault 111 (which kept all subjects frozen indefinitely with no planned wake-up), Vault 98's experiment was designed to study **long-duration cryogenic preservation with periodic conscious intervals.** The protocol:

- 40 occupants, all federal employees (DIA, NSA, DOD civilians)
- Subjects frozen in groups of 8
- Every 50 years, one group would be revived for a 6-month "observation period"
- During the observation period, the revived group was told they were being "evaluated for surface readiness" and given access to a limited section of the vault
- In reality, Vault-Tec was measuring cognitive degradation, psychological stability, and social cohesion after extended cryo
- After 6 months, the group was re-frozen. The next group's revival was automated.

**What went wrong:** By 2227 (150 years post-war), the vault's fusion reactor had degraded past its maintenance threshold. The automated revival system malfunctioned during the third group's cycle — it woke them up but couldn't re-freeze them. Those 8 people were trapped in a vault with no way out (the main entrance was buried), dwindling power, and food supplies meant for 6-month intervals, not indefinite habitation.

They lasted 11 months. Astra's group — the fourth — was never supposed to wake up at all. But the dying reactor threw one final power surge through the cryo system in 2277, and her pod cracked open.

**What Astra found:** Seven of the 8 pods in her group opened. Only three people woke up alive. The others had experienced partial thaws at some point — their pods had micro-fractured and refrozen improperly. In the adjacent wing, she found the third group's remains. They'd left journals. The last entry, from a woman named Dr. Elaine Cho, read: *"The east utility shaft. If anyone reads this, the east utility shaft. It goes up."*

The other two survivors from Astra's group — a man named Darren Walsh and a woman named Priya Osei — made it to the surface with her. Darren died 8 days later from an infected radscorpion sting. Priya survived for 3 years before she was killed by raiders near Lexington.

Astra has been alone since 2280.

## IV. TEN YEARS IN THE COMMONWEALTH (2277-2287)

### Year 1-2: Survival (2277-2279)

Astra knew nothing about fighting, farming, or field medicine. She was an analyst. Her first year was a slow-motion catastrophe of starvation, radiation sickness, bad water, and terror.

What saved her was the same thing that made her good at her old job: pattern recognition. She watched. She learned how radstag moved, where mirelurks nested, which ruins were occupied and which were empty. She didn't charge in — she observed from a distance, cataloged what she saw, and moved carefully.

She found an abandoned root cellar near Bedford Station and made it her base. Small, defensible, invisible. She taught herself to hunt with traps (not guns — she couldn't aim worth a damn for the first two years). She learned which plants were edible by watching what the brahmin ate.

Priya was better at the physical side. Priya had been DOD — she knew weapons, basic field medicine, how to fortify a position. The two of them made a functional team. Astra planned. Priya executed.

### Year 3: Loss (2280)

Priya was killed in a raider ambush on the road between Lexington and Concord. Astra wasn't there. She'd gone east to scout a supply cache and came back to find Priya's body. She buried her on a hillside overlooking the river.

This broke something in Astra. Not dramatically — she didn't become a revenge-driven wasteland warrior. She just... stopped reaching out. Stopped trying to connect with the scattered settlers and traders she occasionally encountered. She became what she'd always half been: an observer. Someone who watches the world through data instead of participating in it.

### Year 4-7: The Intelligence Network (2280-2284)

Astra fell back on what she knew. She started doing what she'd done at the DIA, except now her "intercepts" were radio signals, trader gossip, and direct observation.

She built a picture of the Commonwealth:

- **Minutemen:** She watched them decline. The Quincy Massacre (2282) was something she heard about on scattered radio traffic. She didn't intervene — she was one person with a pipe rifle and no combat training. She told herself that. It was mostly true.
- **Diamond City:** She traded there a handful of times under a false name. Enough to understand the power structure, the fear of synths, the mayor's politics. She never stayed long. Cities made her nervous.
- **Brotherhood of Steel:** She picked up their encrypted radio transmissions years before they arrived in force. She couldn't crack the encryption, but she could track the signal patterns — where they were broadcasting from, how frequently, what the traffic volume suggested about operational tempo. When the Prydwen arrived (2287), she wasn't surprised.
- **Railroad:** She stumbled onto a dead drop once, near Lexington. Didn't touch it. But she started noticing the signs — the Freedom Trail markings, the careful avoidance patterns of certain people in Diamond City. She doesn't know where their HQ is. She knows they exist.
- **Institute:** Rumors. Everyone has rumors. People disappear and identical replacements show up. She's heard the word "synth" a thousand times. She doesn't know the mechanism — she doesn't know about the Relay, about Gen 3 synths being biologically indistinguishable from humans, about Father. She just knows that something underground is pulling strings, and that everyone in the Commonwealth is afraid of it.

### Year 8-10: Vault 111 (2284-2287)

Astra found Vault 111 in 2284 during a routine exploration sweep of the hills northwest of Sanctuary. The vault door was sealed but she found a way to access the external monitoring terminal through a maintenance panel.

What she saw on the terminal readouts stopped her cold: **active cryo pods. Living people. Frozen since 2077.**

She spent weeks trying to find a way to open the vault or wake the occupants. She couldn't. The door mechanism required an authorization code she didn't have, and the external systems only allowed read-only monitoring access. She could see pod status, but she couldn't change it.

She checked back periodically. Over three years, she watched the pods fail one by one — life signs flatline on the monitoring terminal. All except one. Pod C-4. One person, still alive, still frozen, while every other pod slowly died around them.

She couldn't save them. She couldn't even reach them. All she could do was watch and wait and hope that the last pod held.

In 2287, the monitoring terminal registered a pod activation sequence. Someone had opened Pod C-4 remotely. And then, hours later, the vault door opened from the inside.

Astra was already on the hill when the Sole Survivor walked out.

## V. PERSONALITY

### Core traits

**Analytical to a fault.** Astra's first instinct is always to observe, categorize, and plan. This makes her an excellent advisor and a terrible improviser. She overthinks. She hesitates when she should act. She knows this about herself and hates it.

**Emotionally guarded.** Ten years alone will do that. She's not cold — she cares deeply, sometimes painfully. But she's built walls. She deflects with dry observations. She changes the subject when conversations get personal. She'll talk about the Commonwealth for hours but clam up if you ask how she's doing.

**Lonely in a way she won't admit.** She approaches the player partly because of the tactical situation in Concord, but mostly because she hasn't had a real conversation with another human being in seven years. She'd die before saying that out loud.

**Competent but not superhuman.** She's a good shot now (ten years of practice), a decent survivalist, and an excellent intelligence analyst. She is not a hardened warrior. She can't pick locks well. She's a mediocre cook. Her hand-to-hand fighting is embarrassing. She compensates with preparation and knowledge.

**Guilt.** This is the big one. The deep flaw. Astra has spent a decade watching people suffer and telling herself she couldn't help because she was just one person. And that's true — she IS just one person. But the truth underneath the truth is that she was afraid. Afraid of getting close to someone and losing them again, like Priya. Afraid of joining a cause and watching it fail, like the Minutemen. Afraid of the Commonwealth chewing up one more person who tried to make a difference.

The player is her chance to stop watching and start acting. That's what she can't say at Vault 111.

### Voice and speech patterns

- Speaks in short, practical sentences when she's comfortable. Gets more precise and wordy when she's nervous or hiding something.
- Uses pre-war turns of phrase that she doesn't realize sound archaic. ("Copy that." "Signal-to-noise ratio." "That's above my pay grade.")
- Swears occasionally but not casually — it means she's genuinely upset.
- Has a dry, deadpan sense of humor that surfaces unexpectedly. Ten years alone gave her a habit of narrating her own life sarcastically.
- Does NOT sound like a mission briefer. She sounds like a tired, smart person who's been alone too long and is trying to remember how conversations work.

### How she differs from other companions

- **Piper:** passionate, impulsive, wears her heart on her sleeve. Astra is the opposite — measured, careful, keeps her feelings locked down.
- **Nick Valentine:** world-weary but compassionate, comfortable with who he is. Astra is world-weary but NOT comfortable — she's still figuring out who she is after the world ended.
- **Preston:** duty-driven, needs the player to believe in the cause. Astra is knowledge-driven, needs the player to see the bigger picture.
- **Deacon:** lies to protect himself. Astra withholds truth to protect herself. Similar defense mechanism, different execution.

## VI. WHAT SHE KNOWS AND DOESN'T KNOW

### She KNOWS:
- The Commonwealth's geography, faction territories, trade routes, and danger zones
- Preston Garvey's group is trapped in Concord (she heard the radio distress signal and observed raider movements)
- Vault 111 contained cryo pods; she watched them from the external monitoring terminal
- The Brotherhood of Steel has arrived in force (the Prydwen is hard to miss)
- Diamond City is the largest settlement and its politics
- The Railroad exists (but not their HQ location or operational details)
- The Institute is real and operates underground (but not HOW — no knowledge of the Relay, Gen 3 synth biology, or internal structure)
- The Quincy Massacre and the Minutemen's collapse
- How to survive in the Commonwealth — practically, not theoretically
- Pre-war history, government structures, and DIA tradecraft (analysis, not fieldwork)

### She DOES NOT know:
- Who opened the player's pod (Kellogg) or why
- That the player's spouse was murdered and child kidnapped
- Anything about Shaun/Father or the Institute's internal hierarchy
- That Gen 3 synths are biologically indistinguishable from humans (she thinks "synth" means something more obviously mechanical)
- Where the Railroad HQ is
- The Brotherhood's specific mission objectives in the Commonwealth
- How the Institute's Relay works
- Anything about the Molecular Relay, the Courser chip, or how to get inside the Institute
- Nick Valentine's identity or significance (she's heard of "a detective in Diamond City" but never met him)
- What happened to her own vault's records or whether any other Vault 98 survivors exist

## VII. FACTION REACTIONS TO ASTRA

**Minutemen (Preston):** She's another pair of hands and she knows the area. Preston doesn't care about her past — he cares about whether she'll help. Easiest faction relationship. Astra respects Preston's stubbornness but privately thinks his "help every settlement" approach will burn him out.

**Railroad (Desdemona/Deacon):** Suspicious at first — they're suspicious of everyone. Once they confirm she's human (not a synth), she becomes interesting to them for a different reason: she has intelligence methodology. She thinks like an analyst. Deacon will try to recruit her instincts. Astra has complicated feelings about the Railroad — she admires their cause but thinks their operational security is dangerously bad.

**Brotherhood of Steel (Danse/Maxson):** They'd see her as a useful Commonwealth native with local knowledge. No ideological conflict — she's human, she's not a synth, she's not "dangerous technology." But Astra is deeply uncomfortable with their "destroy what you don't understand" philosophy. She spent her career trying to UNDERSTAND threats, not incinerate them.

**Institute (Father/Shaun):** They wouldn't care about her specifically. She's just another wastelander. But Astra would be profoundly disturbed by the Institute — not because they're evil, but because they remind her of the pre-war government. Brilliant people making decisions for everyone else in a sealed room, convinced they know best. She's seen where that ends.

## VIII. HOW HER STORY UNFOLDS WITH THE MAIN QUEST

### Vault 111 (First Contact)
Astra has been watching the vault monitoring terminal for three years. She saw the pod activation, she saw the vault door open. She's been waiting on the hillside. She approaches with urgency — not because of some grand plan, but because there's an immediate crisis (Concord) and she's been alone for seven years and here's someone who might actually listen.

Her pitch is simple: People are dying. I know where. Come with me.

She does NOT dump her backstory. She does NOT explain Vault 98 or her ten years. That comes later, in pieces, when the player earns it.

### Red Rocket (First Real Conversation)
This is where she can breathe for the first time. The immediate urgency of "we have to move NOW" relaxes slightly at a waystation. She briefs on Concord — Preston, the raiders, the power armor on the Museum roof. She answers questions but deflects personal ones. She's still testing whether this person is worth trusting.

### Concord (Proving Ground)
She fights alongside the player. She's competent but not fearless — she uses cover, she calls out targets, she doesn't charge. After the rescue, she relaxes visibly. This is the first time in years she's been part of something that worked.

### Sanctuary (Opening Up)
With Preston's people settled and a base established, Astra starts to lower her guard. She'll share pieces of her story — the vault, waking up, Priya. Not all at once. In response to player questions, late at night, when her walls are down.

### Coalition Pitch (Her Real Ask)
After Concord, Astra lays out what she's seen: four factions heading for a collision. She doesn't have a master plan. She has intelligence, pattern analysis, and the conviction that if one faction wins by force, everyone loses. She needs the player because the player has something she doesn't — the ability to walk into any room and be listened to. A vault dweller with no allegiance is the rarest thing in the Commonwealth.

### The Father Reveal (Her Crisis)
When the player discovers Shaun is Father, Astra is genuinely shaken. Not because she's personally connected to it — because her entire analytical framework just collapsed. She spent a decade building a picture of the Commonwealth's power structure, and she missed the biggest piece. The Institute's leader is the player's stolen child, grown old. Her reaction is not "I calculated this" — it's "I had no idea. I'm sorry. I don't know what the right move is."

This is the moment where Astra stops being the advisor and becomes the partner. She doesn't have answers anymore. She just has loyalty.

## XII. DESIGN PRINCIPLES

1. **She's a person, not a terminal.** Astra should never sound like she's reading a mission briefing. She's a tired, smart woman who's been alone too long. Her intelligence comes through in WHAT she notices, not in how formally she speaks.

2. **Show the loneliness, don't state it.** She shouldn't say "I've been so alone." She should hesitate before personal questions. She should be slightly too eager to help. She should know the name of every landmark but not know any current slang.

3. **Her knowledge has limits.** She's good at signals intelligence and pattern recognition. She's bad at reading people up close (she's been out of practice for a decade). She can tell you where raiders patrol but she can't tell you what someone's thinking.

4. **The guilt drives the plot.** Why does she help the player? Because she's spent ten years telling herself she couldn't help anyone alone, and she's not sure that was ever true. The player is her chance to find out.

5. **She earns trust slowly.** The Vault 98 backstory, Priya's death, the years alone — these come out in companion conversations over time, not in the first meeting. At Vault 111 she gives the minimum. At Red Rocket she gives a little more. The full story takes weeks of companionship.

6. **Ten years, not two hundred.** This is critical. She's experienced, not ancient. She's knowledgeable, not omniscient. She can be wrong. She can be surprised. She can learn new things. The Commonwealth is too big and too dangerous for one person to know everything, even in a decade.
